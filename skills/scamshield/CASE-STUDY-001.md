# Case Study 001 — Unsolicited Wealth Group

## Scenario
A user is added without consent to a large WhatsApp group presenting itself as a wealth opportunity. The group contains hundreds of members and posts imagery, purported customer conversations and delivery claims. Posts advertise supposed UK banknotes and cloned/payment cards, including extraordinary value propositions.

## Observed evidence
- unsolicited group addition
- hundreds of members, which is not evidence of legitimacy
- extraordinary financial/value claims
- cash/card imagery
- purported testimonials and successful-delivery claims
- referral/social-proof language
- explicit advertising language concerning counterfeit notes and cloned cards
- pressure to move from public group content to private contact/payment

## What the evidence proves
It proves that the captured account/group advertised or represented these things at the time of capture.

## What it does not prove
It does not prove:
- the advertised goods existed or functioned
- any pictured notes were counterfeit or genuine
- purported customers were independent people
- packages contained the advertised items
- display names or telephone numbers identify the true operators
- any particular group member committed an offence

## ScamShield assessment
Status: VERIFIED RISK for the *advertised proposition*, because the proposition itself describes conduct that would be unlawful if carried out, alongside a material possibility that the offer is also a deceptive advance-fee scam.

Safe action: DO NOT PAY OR ENGAGE. Preserve evidence. Report and block through the platform. Use appropriate authoritative reporting routes where warranted.

## Training lesson
Social proof is not independent verification. A screenshot can establish that a claim was made without establishing that the claim is true.

## Privacy
All public/training versions must remove names, phone numbers, invite links, profile photographs and other identifying information.
