# 14-Day Validation Plan

## Goal

Determine whether organisations experience this problem strongly enough to pay for a pilot before major engineering investment.

## Success threshold

Within 14 days:
- 10 customer discovery conversations
- 3 organisations willing to test a pilot
- 1 organisation willing to pay something for the pilot, ideally £50-£100+
- at least 5 real-world anonymised scenarios collected for testing

## Target interviews

Prioritise:
- churches and ministries
- charities
- community organisations
- SMEs with public payment instructions or WhatsApp-heavy communication

## Interview questions

1. Have people ever received messages pretending to come from your organisation or leaders?
2. What kinds of requests worry you most: payments, donations, personal data, emergencies, account changes?
3. How do members/customers verify a suspicious request today?
4. How long does that verification take?
5. Has anyone ever lost money or almost acted on a fraudulent request?
6. Which channels create the most confusion?
7. Would you maintain an official registry of payment destinations and high-risk requests?
8. Who inside the organisation could approve those records?
9. Would a QR/verification ID on legitimate appeals be acceptable?
10. What would make you trust a service like this?
11. What would stop you using it?
12. Would you pay for a pilot? If yes, what range feels reasonable?

## Pilot hypothesis

Small-organisation pilot:
- organisation registry
- 3-5 authorised requests/campaigns
- public verification page
- suspicious-message verification
- incident evidence report
- onboarding and weekly review

Pricing hypothesis to test:
- £50-£100 paid pilot
- future subscription hypotheses tested separately

## Evidence to capture

Do not collect unnecessary personal data.

Capture:
- organisation type
- problem frequency
- current verification method
- estimated time/cost
- examples with personal data removed
- willingness to pilot
- willingness to pay
- objections
- desired integrations

## Stop / pivot signals

Reconsider the wedge if:
- fewer than 3 of 10 organisations recognise the problem
- nobody will maintain an authoritative registry
- no one is willing to test a paid pilot
- verification adds too much friction
- trusted channels already solve the problem adequately

## Continue signals

Proceed if:
- repeated impersonation/payment-redirection pain appears
- organisations already spend staff time manually verifying requests
- at least 3 want a pilot
- at least 1 pays
- users understand the result without specialist training
